The data is already collected. Getting through it is the work.

Conservation research generates more imagery, audio, and field data every year than the people doing the work can process. Field seasons keep producing; the archive keeps growing; the questions that motivated the collection wait their turn.

Operational Ecology builds production-grade machine-learning systems that close that gap. The biologists and technicians whose careers are devoted to these populations are the experts; our role is to amplify that work, without replacing or overshadowing it.

Project Fathom

Research · Principal investigator

Four generations of Northern Resident orca have been recorded at OrcaLab since the 1970s: tens of thousands of hours of continuous sound and context that no human ear can revisit across years. Project Fathom asks what is in it: how vocal exchanges are structured, what varies with family and season, and what the whales attend to.

The work is a collaboration with researchers carrying over 130 years of combined orca experience, and it is valuable for what it finds out, not for anything it ships.
